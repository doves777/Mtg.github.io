# 08 · AI Capabilities

## Guiding Principle

For MVP, do **not** make AI the center of the product. Core workflows must be strong on their own; AI is layered in as a differentiator afterward.

## AI for Vendors

- Auto-categorize uploaded inventory.
- Detect duplicate or inconsistent listings.
- Recommend pricing changes.
- Recommend buylist prices and identify cards worth actively buying.
- Identify high-demand cards.
- Suggest which inventory to bring to a show.
- Summarize sales trends after an event.
- Forecast likely demand by show type, region, or season.
- Generate customer-facing descriptions.
- Flag suspicious pricing or margin issues.
- Help employees answer product or pricing questions quickly.

## AI for Customers

- Recommend cards based on interests, teams, players, sets, or past purchases.
- Help customers find cards from a want list.
- Suggest alternatives when a card is unavailable.
- Explain card differences, conditions, or market trends.
- Build a pickup cart from a natural-language request.

> Example: "Find me Pokémon cards under $50 from this vendor that would be good for a Charizard collector."

## AI for Operations

- Demand forecasting.
- Inventory restock suggestions.
- Buylist optimization and acquisition recommendations.
- Show performance summaries.
- Pricing anomaly detection.
- Employee performance insights.
- "What changed since last show?" summaries.

## Best AI MVP Candidates

1. AI inventory cleanup
2. AI pricing recommendations
3. AI sales/event summary
4. AI customer want-list matching
5. AI buylist pricing recommendations
